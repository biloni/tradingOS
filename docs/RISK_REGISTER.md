# Risk Register

Product, technical, data/vendor, and regulatory risks specific to this
refinement. Likelihood/impact are qualitative (Low/Medium/High) — this is a
personal-use planning artifact, not a formal enterprise risk process.
Reviewed and updated whenever a blocking decision (docs/BLOCKING_DECISIONS.md)
is confirmed or a new phase ships.

| # | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation | Owner/trigger to revisit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R-01 | Committee LLM cost exceeds the "Normal" scenario estimate (docs/PROVIDER_MATRIX.md) in practice — real evidence bundles/prompts turn out larger than estimated. | Medium | Low (dollars, not capital-at-risk) | The pre-filter bar (BLOCKING_DECISIONS.md #3) is the direct lever; `LLMCallLog`'s existing per-call cost tracking (unchanged from shipped MVP) makes this visible immediately, not discovered at month-end. | Revisit if a week's actual cost exceeds 2× the "Normal" estimate. |

| R-03 | VIX-via-ETP-proxy (BLOCKING_DECISIONS.md #2) diverges meaningfully from spot VIX in a way that produces a wrong regime classification at a critical moment (e.g. classifies `CALM` during a real volatility spike because the ETP proxy lagged). | Low-Medium | **High** — this is the one risk on this register that touches capital preservation directly, since regime feeds the risk budget (ADR-034). | Regime classification uses a percentile/rate-of-change window (not a single-day snapshot), which smooths most single-day proxy noise; price/breadth confirmation (FR-01) is a second, independent signal that doesn't depend on the VIX proxy at all, so a proxy failure alone can't fully mask a real regime shift. | R-04 | The no-average-down precondition (ADR-037) is too mechanically narrow (e.g. a real new catalyst exists but isn't a timestamped news/earnings item the rule recognizes) and blocks a genuinely good add-on, frustrating the user into working around the system. | Medium | Low-Medium (a false-negative block is annoying, not capital-destructive — the opposite failure mode, a false-positive allow, is the one that would matter, and the rule is deliberately conservative against that) | The rule is intentionally conservative (documented tradeoff in ADR-037) — a blocked add-on can still be logged directly in the trade journal (the journal never blocks anything, only the committee-assisted recommendation path does) if the user genuinely disagrees, preserving principle 11's spirit (human override, journaled and auditable) without weakening the automated guardrail. | Revisit the catalyst-detection rule's specificity if real usage shows a pattern of legitimate blocks. |
| R-05 | Structured-output validation failures (a committee role returns malformed JSON) are more frequent in practice than the "single retry" design assumes, degrading premarket-plan reliability. | Low-Medium | Medium (an incomplete plan is a real usability problem for a "concise premarket plan" product) | docs/MODEL_GOVERNANCE.md's evaluation fixture set (planned) should include deliberately adversarial/edge-case evidence bundles specifically to surface this before it happens live; a persistent per-role failure rate is exactly the drift signal docs/MODEL_GOVERNANCE.md already names as worth watching. | Revisit prompt/schema design for any role with a sustained validation-failure rate. |
| R-06 | Manual trade journal entries drift from reality over time (the user forgets to log a trade, or logs it inaccurately) since there's no automated reconciliation against a real external broker (ADR-039/FR-34). | Medium-High | Medium — degrades performance-dashboard/recommendation-vs-reality accuracy, but doesn't touch actual capital (the user's real broker remains the source of truth for their real money regardless of what this app shows). | Explicitly named as an accepted MVP limitation (FR-34) rather than solved — a future broker-statement-import feature is a real Phase-2 candidate if this proves a persistent problem, not attempted now without a demonstrated need. | Revisit if the user reports the journal regularly diverging from their real account. |
| R-07 | The in-process scheduler (ADR-040) misses a scheduled run (e.g. the machine is asleep/off at 6am, or the process crashed and wasn't restarted). | Medium-High (a personal machine isn't guaranteed to be on/awake at a fixed premarket time) | Low-Medium (a missed plan is inconvenient, not capital-destructive — no autonomous action depends on the schedule firing) | The Premarket Plan page's empty-state design (docs/UX_MAP.md) already accounts for this — shows yesterday's plan plus a manual "run now" action, never a blank page; this is a UX mitigation for a real, accepted operational limitation of a single-machine personal deployment, not something the scheduler choice itself needs to solve. | Revisit if this proves frequent enough to be genuinely disruptive — options at that point include OS-level wake scheduling or moving the scheduled job to a small always-on host, both bigger changes than this pass should pre-commit to. |
| R-08 | Prompt-injection via evidence content (news headlines) attempts to manipulate a committee role (docs/THREAT_MODEL.md boundary 2). | Low-Medium (requires the specific headline text to exist in fetched evidence — not something an attacker directly controls, unlike a form submission) | Low, **by design** — the structural mitigation (numeric outputs always echoed from deterministic tool results, never taken from model text) means even a successful manipulation of a role's narrative can't produce an actual out-of-policy action, only a misleading sentence a human would read before acting anyway. | See docs/THREAT_MODEL.md boundary 2 for the full mitigation; the residual risk is "a misleading narrative," not "an unauthorized action," which is the correct residual risk shape for this kind of system. | Revisit if evidence sources ever expand to less-curated content (e.g. social media, forums) — headline-from-a-licensed-news-vendor is a meaningfully lower-risk input than open user-generated content. |
| R-09 | Regulatory/compliance exposure from providing structured BUY/SELL/HOLD recommendations, even for a personal paper-trading tool. | Low (single user, no live trading, no distribution to others, explicitly framed as decision support not advice throughout the product) | Low-Medium if ever misjudged | Every recommendation surface (UI, premarket plan, committee narrative) carries the existing "decision support only, not investment advice, final call is yours" framing already established in the shipped MVP's `/ask` system prompt (docs/TEST_EVIDENCE.md Phase 4) — extended to every new surface, not just `/ask`. This remains a personal tool for its single user; it is explicitly never distributed, sold, or exposed to other users (ADR-007) — that boundary is the actual control, not the disclaimer text alone. | Revisit immediately and re-evaluate this entire risk row if multi-user support or any form of sharing/distribution is ever considered — out of scope today (docs/MVP_PLAN.md). |
| R-10 | Scope creep — the refined product (15 capabilities, 8-role committee, 6 new bounded contexts) is a large surface for a personal, one-developer project, risking a half-finished implementation. | Medium | Medium — directly contradicts PROJECT_INSTRUCTIONS.md's "depth over breadth" acceptance philosophy. | docs/MVP_PLAN.md's explicit MVP/Phase-2/Future tiering exists specifically to manage this — sentiment scoring, calibration, walk-forward, and push alerts are already named as deliberately deferred rather than attempted alongside everything else.
